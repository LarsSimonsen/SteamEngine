A lot has happened since the last update. Most of the recent work has focused on making the calculators easier to use and connecting them more closely with the growing battery, mod, board, and material catalogs.

Battery packs and mod presets

The Battery Drain calculator now supports multi-cell configurations, including series and parallel arrangements. It calculates the load per cell and gives estimates for current, power, runtime, voltage sag, and safety headroom using more conservative voltage assumptions near the mod’s cutoff point.

You can also select a mod as a preset. When its battery configuration is known, Steam Engine can fill in the number of cells, their arrangement, supported cell formats, power limits, and relevant operating mode.

The presets include devices ranging from older ProVari models to newer regulated mods such as the Lost Vape Centaurus DNA250C. Control boards and the mods that use them are also linked together in the catalog.

As always, presets are a convenient starting point—not a substitute for checking the device manual, cell specifications, and the condition of your batteries.

Better comparison tools

The catalog now includes dedicated comparison pages for batteries, materials, and mods.

Battery comparisons make it easier to inspect capacity, discharge ratings, dimensions, weight, and other identifying details. Material comparisons include resistance and temperature-response curves, while mod comparisons show specifications and power curves where sufficient information is available.

Catalog search has also become more forgiving. It can now find likely matches despite minor spelling differences, punctuation, spacing, or model-name variations.

A more useful mod catalog

Mod profiles can now describe more than their headline wattage. Where the information is available, they include battery configuration, supported cell formats, compatible control boards, input-voltage ranges, output modes, and operating features.

This information is shared with the calculators rather than being copied into separate preset lists. That should make the catalog more consistent and allow future corrections to benefit several parts of the site at once.

There is still plenty of research left to do, particularly for older or poorly documented devices. If you spot an incorrect specification or have a reliable source for missing information, please let me know.

Calculator improvements

The Mod Range calculator has received a clearer graph and a more compact layout. Its results can now be carried directly into Battery Drain, along with the selected mod and maximum power, making it easier to continue from a mod’s electrical limits to a battery-load estimate.

There have also been many smaller usability improvements across the other calculators:

  • Coil wrapping accepts sub-ohm values more naturally and presents its results more clearly.
  • Ohm’s law has better field interactions, inline validation, and separate reset controls.
  • Wire Wizard has improved desktop and mobile layouts, especially for composite wires and temperature points.
  • E-liquid recipes preserve editable values more reliably.
  • The converter has clearer input, result, and unit columns.
  • Electrical inputs now use more consistent increments across calculators.

Most of these are small changes on their own, but together they should make the site feel less awkward—particularly on phones.

More languages

Greek, Turkish, Indonesian, and Latin American Spanish have joined the languages already available on the site. The existing translations have also gone through a substantial round of corrections, with particular attention paid to technical terminology.

Machine-assisted translation can still produce mistakes, so corrections from fluent speakers are very welcome.

Behind the scenes

Steam Engine’s frontend and calculation services have also moved to Cloudflare. The calculation interfaces are now versioned and validated, which provides a safer foundation for changing the implementation without unexpectedly changing saved or cached results.

There is more work ahead, but the direction is becoming clearer: keep the calculators practical and familiar, improve the data behind them, and make the site easier to maintain and contribute to.
